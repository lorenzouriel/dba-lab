# Appendix D: Suggested Reading

Quick-reference note: the author's reading list for going deeper into database design and SQL. Grouped as in the book.

## Database Books

1. Connolly, Thomas & Carolyn Begg — *Database Systems: A Practical Approach to Design, Implementation, and Management* (6th ed., 2014).
2. Coronel, Carlos & Steven Morris — *Database Systems: Design, Implementation, and Management* (11th ed., 2015).
3. Date, C.J. — *An Introduction to Database Systems* (8th ed., 2003).
4. Date, C.J. — *Database in Depth: Relational Theory for Practitioners* (2005).
5. Date, C.J. — *Database Design and Relational Theory: Normal Forms and All That Jazz* (2012).
6. Hernandez, Michael J. — *Database Design for Mere Mortals* (3rd ed., 2013) — companion volume to this book, covers the design side rather than the querying side.

## Books on SQL

1. Bowman, Judith S., Sandra L. Emerson & Marcy Darnovsky — *The Practical SQL Handbook: Using SQL Variants* (4th ed., 2001).
2. Celko, Joe — *Joe Celko's SQL for Smarties: Advanced SQL Programming* (5th ed., 2014).
3. Date, C.J. & Hugh Darwen — *A Guide to the SQL Standard* (4th ed., 1996).
4. Rockoff, Larry — *The Language of SQL* (2nd ed., 2016).
5. Viescas, John L., Douglas J. Steele & Ben G. Clothier — *Effective SQL: 61 Ways to Write Better SQL* (2016) — by the same author, a natural next step after finishing this book.

✨ **Side learning:** the two "Mere Mortals" books (this one and *Database Design for Mere Mortals*) are meant as a pair — Hernandez covers modeling/normalization, Viescas covers querying the result.
